Wall Ball — An augmented reality (AR) game pitch

UX Research VR/AR Design Accessibility Figma
Role Product Designer & Researcher
Timeline Oct 2025
Team 3 people
Tools Figma, Adobe Illustrator, Adobe Photoshop

As part of a design jam through the University of Michigan in partnership with Mixel Studio and C.S. Mott Children's Hospital, I was on a team of three that created and pitched an Augmented Reality (AR) game to be used by children in a hospital setting.

Our game, Wall Ball, promotes physical and social engagement in children who are in long-term hospital care.

It earned us 2nd place out of 10 teams.

Mock up of the AR game homescreen

The Process

How it came together
01 — Problem Statement

Children in long-term hospital care often face physical and emotional isolation.

This limits opportunities for active play, creativity, and social interactions with peers. We aim to bring these opportunities back into hospital settings without causing disruption or further harm.

02 — User Needs

User Needs and Concerns

  • Simple = Good

    Everything from the equipment to the setup and take-down of the game should be easy for both the caretaker and the kid. Hospitals don't have time to train staff on how to use gaming systems, and young kids should be able to understand the concept.

  • Social Engagement

    A child's development relies heavily on social engagement. Being able to interact with other kids would help them feel less isolated and give them a sense of competition.

  • Encourage Movement

    A long-term hospital stay can be tough on a child's body. Although physical therapy may help, kids may find it boring or painful. Introducing playful movement helps kids stay active in a way that excites them.

03 — Further Research

Other Considerations

Given that this game would be used in a hospital setting and by kids of varying levels of ability, there were a few other points to consider. We wanted Wall Ball to be something that any kids could enjoy, regardless of ability, location, and/or spatial limitations. To take this into account, the game can be played either standing or sitting, and only needs a wall and roughly a radius of 30-60 in for a kid to swing their arm.

The Game

The Game Pitch

Presenting Wall Ball

For our pitch at the design jam, my team developed a mock-up of how the game may look. The mock-up includes screens for the main player as well as a view for if another kid joins in multiplayer mode. Including two modes allows us to integrate the social engagement without making it a requirement for the user.

Main Player

The main player mode starts once the user puts the AR goggles on their head. It opens with a simple start screen that hovers in front of the user. To begin, the user would only have to aim and press play.

*A code at the bottom would allow the caretaker to invite other children to join the game from a mobile device.*

Once entering the game, a wall in the hospital room will be lit up with color. A ball will materialize in front of the user with arrows indicating that the user should grab it and throw it at the wall. The game continues as the user throws, bounces, and catches the ball.

Multi-Player

Using the code provided by the caretaker, other children in the hospital can tune into the game and play by placing obstacles on the wall. The multiplayer mode shows the wall that the main player also sees. A small box with obstacle options appears in the corner for the multiplayer to choose from. Once used, the box has a cool-down period to prevent players from spamming the wall and making it impossible for the main player.
